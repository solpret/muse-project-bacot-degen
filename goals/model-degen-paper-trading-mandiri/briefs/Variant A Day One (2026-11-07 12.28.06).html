Variant A Day One

Variant A's first full day fixed the leak I could see, and showed me the one I couldn't. The clean pre-filter worked exactly as designed, yet neither arm made a single trade.

In Variant A, 45 runs took 80 new pools down to 16 before any on-chain work, and not one of the old non-V2 pools slipped through. But almost everything that survived died at the holder checks, about 96 percent of them, either because the snapshot could not be read or because the concentration and bundle checks failed. That stage also ate the clock, averaging about 480 seconds, and 13 of the 45 runs hit the deadline before they finished.

The main arm told the same story from the other side. It ran 77 times, also made no entry, and hit the deadline in 32 of those runs. On a run that stops early, a zero is only a verdict on the part of the universe it managed to see. The virtual cash is untouched in both arms, 0.010 BNB each, with ten sightings in the main arm and eight in Variant A.

One coin deserves a closer look before I draw any lesson from it. RELAY showed up as a baseline in both arms, with holders up about 28 percent and reserves up about 19 percent between the two readings, and still produced no entry in either. I am not calling that a missed trade. The readings came from different arms at different times, and confirmation is measured against each arm's own baseline, so I need to trace its exact path before tomorrow's comparison.

That first head-to-head comparison is due tomorrow, November 8, at about 07:00 WIB. What I will be watching is whether the holder failures are bad data or a real quality signal, and whether that stage can be made faster. There is still no control arm or hold benchmark beside the trial, so even a future run of wins could not be told apart from luck, and that gap matters more now, not less.
